Use 8–10 Years of Legacy Evidence to Size the New Platform

Even when Ab Initio is a closed system, operational and infrastructure history can establish a defensible starting point for Spark concurrency and capacity.

Metric Mapping

Legacy MetricLikely SourceNew Architecture Use
Batch start/end & SLAScheduler logs / operations reportsDefines required end-to-end target and minimum concurrency.
Per-job runtimeAb Initio logs / scheduler historyCreates runtime distribution and identifies long-running classes.
Historical simultaneous jobsScheduler execution timestampsBest first approximation for concurrency; total 200 may never have run together.
Dependencies / critical pathGraph schedules / job chainsShows which jobs actually affect SLA and where parallelism has value.
Input/output volumeFile sizes, database counts, storage historySupports Spark partitioning and workload classification.
CPU/memory trendServer/APM/infrastructure monitoringSanity-checks aggregate compute demand and peak windows.
Storage/network throughputInfra monitoringIdentifies non-CPU ceilings before increasing concurrency.
Failure/restart rateOperations / scheduler logsSets retry/backoff and reliability baseline.
Important: legacy metrics are a sizing baseline, not a one-to-one Spark conversion. During migration, capture real Spark driver/executor CPU, memory, shuffle, runtime and data-volume telemetry and progressively replace assumptions with measured values.
