APPROACH 3 • FUTURE TARGET

Policy-Driven Elastic Execution Platform — Airflow + Queue + Kubernetes

Airflow keeps DAG/workflow semantics, while workload admission and execution capacity become independently governed. This is the evolution path when the platform becomes multi-domain, high-volume or SLA/priority driven.

Advantages

  • Decouples workflow arrival from compute availability.
  • Dynamic concurrency based on SLA, priority, capacity and downstream health.
  • Supports multi-domain fairness, quotas and workload classes.
  • Queue absorbs large bursts without forcing immediate pod/node explosion.
  • Strong long-term path for thousands of ETL jobs and specialized workloads.

Trade-offs

  • Additional platform components and operational ownership.
  • Requires durable job contract, idempotency and stronger observability.
  • More design effort around queue semantics, fairness, retry and reconciliation.
  • Potential over-engineering for the first migration wave.

When to Choose / Evolve

  • Multiple applications/domains share the execution platform.
  • Job bursts materially exceed steady-state capacity.
  • Different SLA/priority tiers require admission policy.
  • Airflow pools become difficult to govern globally.
  • Need back-pressure from databases/storage/APIs or cost-aware scheduling.
